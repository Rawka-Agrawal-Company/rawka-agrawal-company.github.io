title: "Income Tax | Jewellery Cannot Be Treated as Unexplained Solely for Exceeding CBDT Limits: ITAT Delhi"
date: 2026-11-11 00:53:57
layout: single
---

In a significant ruling, the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) Delhi has held that gold jewellery discovered during search operations cannot be treated as an unexplained investment under Section 69A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, solely because the quantity exceeds the thresholds prescribed under CBDT Instruction No. 1916. The Tribunal clarified that the CBDT instruction—which outlines non-seizure allowances of 500 grams for married females, 250 grams for unmarried females, and 100 grams for male members—is an administrative guideline governing the conduct of search teams, rather than a rigid statutory limit for tax assessment. Consequently, holding jewellery in excess of these benchmark figures does not automatically render the excess portion unexplained or justify arbitrary additions.

Emphasizing customary Indian practices, the Tribunal noted that the acquisition of jewellery through inheritance, *stridhan*, and customary gifting over extended periods is standard across households. Assessing officers are required to evaluate the taxpayer’s socio-economic background, family traditions, duration of marriage, and plausible accumulation patterns rather than adopting a mechanical approach based strictly on administrative circulars. For tax professionals and taxpayers, this ruling reinforces the principle that CBDT possession limits cannot be used as an absolute ceiling to establish undisclosed income without independent corroborative evidence of unaccounted investment.
