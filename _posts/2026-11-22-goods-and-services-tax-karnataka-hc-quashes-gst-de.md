In a significant development for indirect tax jurisprudence, the High Court of Karnataka has set aside GST demand orders and consequential recovery notices issued by the Revenue, remanding the matter back to the adjudicating authority for a fresh hearing. The Court observed that the impugned orders suffered from procedural infirmities, primarily the failure to afford the taxpayer a meaningful opportunity of being heard—a clear breach of the principles of natural justice and the statutory mandate under Section 75(4) of the CGST/KGST Act, 2017. Recognizing that substantial administrative orders culminating in severe recovery proceedings cannot be sustained without adequate adherence to due process, the Court intervened to prevent manifest injustice and premature coercive actions.

The ruling underscores the indispensable nature of natural justice in GST adjudication, emphasizing that tax authorities cannot bypass the statutory requirement of a personal hearing prior to passing adverse ex-parte assessment orders. By directing a de novo adjudication, the High Court granted the petitioner liberty to file a detailed reply to the initial show-cause notice and mandated that the proper officer pass a reasoned order within a prescribed timeframe. For taxpayers and tax practitioners, this judgment serves as an important precedent against arbitrary demand confirmations and reinforces the judicial expectation that administrative efficiency cannot supersede fair administrative procedure.
