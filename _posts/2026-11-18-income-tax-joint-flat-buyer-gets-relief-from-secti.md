In a significant ruling concerning real estate transactions, the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) has granted relief to a joint property owner by deleting additions made under Section 69 read with Section 115BBE of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The dispute arose after the Assessing Officer treated a proportionate share of the property’s purchase consideration as unexplained investment in the hands of the assessee, merely because the assessee's name appeared as a co-purchaser in the registered sale deed. The appellate authority held that where the primary buyer has fully accounted for the entire investment through disclosed banking channels and accepted the source in their own tax returns, no addition can be sustained against the secondary co-owner whose name was incorporated primarily for convenience, financing eligibility, or family security.

This determination reinforces the settled legal principle that additions under Section 69 must be anchored in demonstrable, unexplained financial contributions rather than technical co-ownership on paper. By nullifying the additions, the tribunal also extinguished the application of the penal tax and surcharge rates under Section 115BBE. For tax practitioners and taxpayers, this ruling highlights the necessity of maintaining a robust paper trail establishing the actual flow of funds and beneficial ownership in joint acquisitions, ensuring that non-contributing co-owners are protected from arbitrary assessments under anti-abuse provisions.
