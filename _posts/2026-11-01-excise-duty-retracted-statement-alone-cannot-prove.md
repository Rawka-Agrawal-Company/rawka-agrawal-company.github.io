---

In a significant ruling, the Kolkata Bench of the Customs, Excise, and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT) has held that allegations of clandestine manufacture and clearance cannot be sustained solely on the basis of a retracted statement without independent, tangible corroboration. Setting aside the duty demand and associated penalties, the Tribunal reiterated that clandestine removal is a serious charge that requires conclusive evidence rather than assumptions or uncorroborated admissions. The Revenue is obligated to substantiate such charges with demonstrable proof, such as the unaccounted procurement of raw materials, disproportionate utility consumption, transport records, and illicit financial flows, especially when a statement recorded under Section 14 of the Central Excise Act, 1944, has been retracted by the assessee.

This judgment reinforces the settled legal principle that an admission, once retracted, loses its standalone evidentiary value and cannot form the sole bedrock of tax evasion demands. For manufacturers and tax practitioners, the ruling provides critical support against arbitrary and presumption-based assessments. It affirms that the burden of proof rests squarely on the Department to establish the charge of clandestine clearance through comprehensive investigation and corroborative material, rather than relying merely on confessional statements obtained during search or inquiry proceedings.
