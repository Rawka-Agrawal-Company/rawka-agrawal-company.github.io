The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) has imposed a penalty of ₹1 lakh on Lite Bite Foods Pvt. Ltd. for levying a mandatory service charge deceptively categorized as a "Staff Contribution" on customer bills. The regulatory authority held that disguising service charges under alternate nomenclature directly violates the July 2022 CCPA Guidelines, which explicitly prohibit hotels and restaurants from levying service charges automatically or mandatorily. Reclassifying the charge as a voluntary contribution while denying consumers the discretion to opt out was ruled as an unfair trade practice and a violation of consumer rights under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

For businesses in the hospitality and retail sectors, this ruling underscores the increasing regulatory scrutiny surrounding deceptive billing and billing transparency. Chartered Accountants and corporate advisors must emphasize to clients that only statutory levies—such as Goods and Services Tax (GST)—can be mandatorily collected on commercial invoices. Any supplementary fee, gratuity, or staff welfare contribution must be entirely optional and explicitly approved by the consumer. Establishments must promptly audit their point-of-sale (POS) systems, invoice templates, and pricing disclosures to ensure strict adherence to consumer protection norms and mitigate exposure to financial penalties and reputational risk.
