title: "Income Tax | ITAT Deletes ₹49.24 Crore Transfer Pricing Adjustments on Franchise Fees and Intra-Group Services"
date: 2026-10-30 11:00:23
layout: single
---

In a significant ruling for multinational enterprises, the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) deleted a Transfer Pricing (TP) adjustment of ₹49.24 crore concerning franchise fees and intra-group corporate support services paid to an Associated Enterprise (AE). The Revenue had challenged the arm’s length pricing of these transactions, largely questioning the commercial necessity of the services and seeking to benchmark the intra-group payments at ‘NIL’ on the grounds that the taxpayer failed the conventional "benefit test." Overruling the Transfer Pricing Officer’s (TPO) approach, the Tribunal reaffirmed that tax authorities cannot step into the shoes of a businessman to determine the commercial expediency or business utility of an expenditure, provided the taxpayer satisfies the threshold requirement of proving actual rendition and receipt of services.

This decision strongly reinforces the settled legal principle that the TPO's mandate is restricted to determining the arm’s length price under the prescribed methods under Section 92C of the Income-tax Act, 1961, rather than questioning the commercial rationale of bona fide business transactions. By validating the taxpayer's cost-allocation keys, underlying service agreements, and contemporaneous proof of service delivery, the ITAT emphasized that intra-group cost-sharing arrangements cannot be arbitrarily disallowed through subjective evaluations. For corporate taxpayers and cross-border entities, this ruling highlights the critical necessity of maintaining robust, granular documentation—including detailed service logs, allocation frameworks, and comprehensive benchmarking studies—to successfully withstand aggressive TP scrutiny.
