**ITAT Hyderabad Denies Section 54F Exemption of ₹1.80 Crore for Unproven Uninhabitable Villas**

The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Hyderabad Bench, has upheld the denial of a capital gains deduction amounting to ₹1.80 crore under Section 54F of the Income-tax Act, 1961, due to the assessee owning more than one residential house on the date of transfer of the original asset. The assessee contended that the existing properties—two villas—were incomplete, semi-finished, and uninhabitable, thereby arguing they should not be classified as "residential houses" for the purposes of the statutory disqualification under the proviso to Section 54F(1). However, the Tribunal rejected this contention, observing that the assessee failed to furnish cogent documentary evidence, such as municipal occupancy reports, valuation records, or independent technical certifications, to substantiate the claim that the premises were unfit for human habitation.

This ruling underscores the stringent burden of proof placed on taxpayers when seeking to exclude existing built-up residential structures from the purview of Section 54F restrictions. The Tribunal reaffirmed that mere subjective assertions regarding the physical or habitable condition of a property will not suffice to bypass statutory exclusions. For tax practitioners and taxpayers, this decision highlights the critical necessity of maintaining contemporaneous objective evidence—including structural engineer reports, municipal completion status, and utility connection records—prior to claiming capital gains exemptions where multiple residential titles are held on the date of transfer.
