The High Court of Delhi has ruled that the Transfer Pricing Officer (TPO) and the Dispute Resolution Panel (DRP) cannot undertake a fresh benchmarking exercise while arbitrarily overlooking the assessee's substantive objections to the rejection of its Transfer Pricing (TP) study. Emphasizing the statutory framework under Section 92C of the Income-tax Act, 1961, the Court held that the documentation and economic analysis maintained by the taxpayer serve as the primary baseline for determining the Arm’s Length Price (ALP). Consequently, revenue authorities cannot bypass the taxpayer's established methodology or introduce alternative comparables without passing a speaking order that demonstrably addresses why the existing TP documentation is invalid, unreliable, or non-compliant with prescribed rules.

This judgment reinforces the requirement of procedural fairness and reasoned adjudication in transfer pricing assessments, preventing tax authorities from making unilateral adjustments through blanket rejections of functional analyses and screening filters. For multinational enterprises and tax practitioners, the ruling provides strong judicial support against arbitrary TP additions, emphasizing that authorities bear the burden of dislodging the taxpayer's analysis on merit before substituting their own. It underscores the critical necessity for corporate taxpayers to maintain robust, contemporaneous TP documentation and systematically counter any proposed rejections with granular, point-by-point objections during assessment proceedings.
