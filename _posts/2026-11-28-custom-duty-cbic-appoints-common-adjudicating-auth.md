The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) has exercised its statutory powers under the Customs Act, 1962, to appoint a Common Adjudicating Authority for multiple Show Cause Notices (SCNs) issued to M/s Actia Automotive India Pvt. Ltd. This administrative measure is invoked when an assessee faces proceedings across multiple customs jurisdictions, ports, or investigating directorates (such as the Directorate of Revenue Intelligence). By vesting adjudication powers in a single designated customs officer, the CBIC aims to prevent conflicting legal determinations, eliminate procedural duplicity, and streamline the quasi-judicial process across all pending notices.

From a tax controversy and corporate advisory perspective, this consolidation significantly mitigates procedural friction and reduces compliance overheads for the taxpayer. A unified adjudication forum allows the entity to present a consistent factual and legal defense, avoiding fragmented proceedings before separate commissionerates. Importers and practitioners handling multi-locational customs disputes should treat this as a standard procedural alignment, ensuring that responses, documentary evidence, and cross-jurisdictional reconciliation statements are structured cohesively before the newly designated authority.
