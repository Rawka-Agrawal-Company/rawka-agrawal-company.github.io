The Supreme Court of India has held that the Central Government’s Motor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines are advisory in nature and not legally binding on state governments. Clarifying the statutory framework under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, the apex court affirmed that state governments retain the constitutional and regulatory autonomy to formulate their own transport policies, establish licensing conditions, or restrict commercial two-wheeler operations within their jurisdictions. Consequently, mobility aggregator platforms cannot mandate state transport authorities to grant operating permissions solely on the basis of central guidelines in the absence of enacted state-level rules.

This ruling has significant compliance and operational ramifications for gig-economy stakeholders, ride-hailing aggregators, and tech-enabled logistics platforms operating across India. In the absence of a preemptive federal mandate, businesses in the shared-mobility sector must navigate a fragmented, state-by-state regulatory landscape. From a corporate governance and risk management standpoint, entities utilizing two-wheelers for passenger transit must align their operational models with specific state transport notifications to avoid regulatory penalties, vehicle impoundments, and operational injunctions.
