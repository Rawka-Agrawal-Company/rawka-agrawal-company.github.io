In a significant ruling concerning customs tariff classification, the Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT), Kolkata, held that the incorporation of a built-in FM radio feature does not alter the fundamental classification of headphones under the Customs Tariff Act, 1975. The dispute centered on whether multi-functional headphones with radio-receiving capabilities should be categorized under Customs Tariff Heading (CTH) 8518 as "Headphones and Earphones" or reclassified under CTH 8527 as "Reception apparatus for radio-broadcasting." Resolving the contention in favor of the assessee, the Tribunal determined that the product retains its primary identity and functionality as an audio listening device under Heading 8518.

The Tribunal’s decision relied on the General Rules for the Interpretation (GRI) of the First Schedule, specifically GRI Rule 3(b), which governs the classification of composite goods consisting of different components. CESTAT observed that the "essential character" of the imported item is derived from its design and utility as headphones, whereas the FM reception feature serves merely as an incidental, secondary function. This ruling offers critical clarity for electronics importers and manufacturers, reaffirming that auxiliary features do not warrant reclassification to higher-tariff headings when the primary commercial utility and consumer perception of the product remain unchanged.
