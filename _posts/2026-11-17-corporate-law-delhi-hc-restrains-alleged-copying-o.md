In a significant development concerning intellectual property rights in legal and professional publishing, the Delhi High Court has granted an ad-interim injunction restraining eight publications and digital portals from copying, reproducing, or distributing the proprietary headnotes, editorial summaries, and case digests curated by Taxmann Allied Services. The plaintiff demonstrated that the defendants had engaged in systematic copyright infringement by lifting verbatim or substantially similar headnotes, structuring, and editorial inputs rather than independently compiling court orders from the public domain. Satisfied that Taxmann established a strong *prima facie* case and that irreparable harm would ensue without immediate relief, the Court restrained the named entities from utilizing this proprietary content across both digital platforms and print editions.

This ruling reaffirms the legal benchmark established in *Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak*, which held that while judicial decisions are matters of public record, the application of independent skill, labor, and professional judgment in preparing headnotes, summaries, and editorial notes constitutes an "original literary work" protected under the Copyright Act, 1957. For tax professionals, research firms, and legal-tech aggregators, this order underscores the legal sanctity of proprietary databases and establishes a firm deterrence against the unauthorized extraction, scraping, and commercial exploitation of specialized professional commentary.
