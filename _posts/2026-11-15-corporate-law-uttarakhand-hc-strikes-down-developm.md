In a significant ruling for industrial processors, the High Court of Uttarakhand has struck down the levy of Development Cess on agricultural produce imported from other states for captive consumption in manufacturing. Adjudicating on the statutory scope of the Uttarakhand Agricultural Produce Marketing (Regulation) Act, the Court held that the State Agricultural Produce Marketing Board and local Market Committees lack the authority to impose development cess or market fees on produce brought into the state solely as raw material. The Bench clarified that the levy is strictly contingent upon an actual transaction of sale or purchase within the designated market area, and the mere cross-border transit of goods for industrial processing does not constitute a taxable market event.

This verdict provides substantial financial and operational relief to manufacturing, FMCG, and agro-processing units operating within Uttarakhand that rely on inter-state raw material procurement. The ruling re-establishes the fundamental legal principle that Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) regulations are meant to govern commercial market transactions, not captive industrial consumption. Businesses and corporate entities that have received adverse demand notices or deposited development cess under protest should re-evaluate their tax exposure, verify compliance procedures, and evaluate appropriate legal avenues for dispute resolution or refund claims.
