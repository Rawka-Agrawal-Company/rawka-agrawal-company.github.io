In a significant ruling clarifying the statutory boundaries of revenue powers, the Hon’ble Bombay High Court held that provisional attachments levied under Section 83 of the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017, automatically cease to have effect after the statutory period of one year. The Court underscored that Section 83(2) contains an explicit legislative sunset clause designed to limit the duration of this pre-adjudication, drastic remedy. Consequently, once a period of one year elapses from the date the attachment order was passed under Section 83(1), the freeze on the taxpayer’s bank accounts or property dissolves by operation of law, requiring no subsequent formal release order from the department to render it invalid.

This judicial interpretation serves as a crucial check on the arbitrary prolonged freezing of operational assets, which frequently disrupts business liquidity and working capital. The judgment establishes that tax authorities cannot treat a provisional attachment as an open-ended encumbrance, nor can banks continue to restrain accounts on the basis of an expired order without a valid, fresh attachment order compliant with statutory conditions. For tax professionals and businesses, this precedent provides immediate legal recourse to approach banking institutions and jurisdictional officers to mandate the unfettered operation of accounts once the one-year statutory threshold is breached.
